Written by Dr. Dennis T. Norton, Jr.  ·  Beta 1.2  ·  January 2015

Converted to HTML/JS single-page app from the original PyQt5 application.

Planck's Law

Planck's law describes the spectral radiance emitted by a blackbody in thermal equilibrium at temperature T. Per unit wavelength (energy units):

B_λ(λ, T) = (2hc² / λ⁵) · 1 / (exp(hc / λk_B T) − 1) [W·m⁻²·sr⁻¹·m⁻¹]

Displayed units in this app are converted to W/cm²/sr/μm (divide by 10¹⁰).

Photon Radiance

The photon spectral radiance replaces the energy per photon hν, giving:

N_λ(λ, T) = (2c / λ⁴) · 1 / (exp(hc / λk_B T) − 1) [photons·s⁻¹·m⁻²·sr⁻¹·m⁻¹]

Displayed in photons/s/cm²/sr/μm.

Physical Constants

h = 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s (Planck constant) c = 299 792 458 m/s (speed of light) k = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K (Boltzmann constant)

In-Band Radiance

Integrated via 200-point composite Simpson's rule over the specified band [λ₁, λ₂]. Result in W/cm²/sr (or photons/s/cm²/sr).

FWHM

Half-maximum crossings are found by linear interpolation on the 2000-point log-spaced spectral curve.

Apparent Temperature Calculator

Computes in-band integrated radiance at 0.5 K increments from 1 K to 6000 K using 60-point Simpson's rule. Forward (T → radiance) and inverse (radiance → T) lookups use linear interpolation on this precomputed curve.

BB Calculator tab: temperature slider (50–6000 K), FWHM auto-band locking, manual band limits, spectral radiance curve plot with shaded integration region.
ApT Calculator tab: hover over the curve for live T/radiance readout; use Lookup section for precise conversions.
